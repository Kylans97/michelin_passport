 On 25 November 2026, the MICHELIN Guide will unveil its inaugural selection for Hunan Province, shining a spotlight on one of China's most distinctive culinary destinations.

 The first Hunan selection will spotlight restaurants across Changsha, Hengyang, and Yiyang, offering a window into the province's rich gastronomic heritage and dynamic dining culture. The MICHELIN Guide Restaurant Ceremony Hunan Province 2027 will be held in Changsha. Renowned for its bold flavors, rich cultural heritage, and vibrant dining scene, Hunan offers travelers a compelling introduction to the diversity of Chinese gastronomy. While the province's spectacular landscapes and historic landmarks have long attracted visitors, its cuisine remains one of the most authentic ways to experience the character and spirit of the region.
 As Gwendal Poullennec, International Director of MICHELIN Guide, notes: “Changsha stands out for the vitality of its food culture. From longstanding local traditions to a new generation of culinary talent, the city offers travelers a compelling way to discover Hunan through its cuisine.”
 Changsha City View © Hunan Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism
 Changsha: The Pulse of Modern HunanTo understand Hunan today, there is perhaps no better place to begin than Changsha. The provincial capital has emerged as one of China's most influential food cities, attracting visitors not only for what they can see, but for what they can taste. Here, the boundaries between dining, social life, and local identity are often blurred. Restaurants are gathering places, street stalls become institutions, and conversations frequently revolve around where to eat next. The city's culinary energy is unmistakable. Throughout the day, diners move between traditional eateries, bustling food streets, contemporary restaurants, and late-night dining venues that remain lively long after sunset. Local specialties showcase the hallmark characteristics of Xiang cuisine: vivid flavors, aromatic chilies, and a deep appreciation for ingredients prepared at their seasonal peak.Yet Changsha's appeal extends beyond the table. The broad waters of the Xiang River shape the city's landscape, while landmarks such as Orange Isle and the historic Yuelu Academy offer insight into the cultural traditions that have influenced generations of Hunan scholars, writers, and thinkers. Together, they create a setting in which food, history, and contemporary urban life exist in constant dialogue. What distinguishes Changsha is not any single attraction or signature dish, but the atmosphere created when all these elements come together. The city possesses a confidence that feels distinctly Hunan: vibrant without being hurried, deeply connected to its traditions while remaining open to new ideas and influences. 
 Yuelu Mountain, Changsha. © Hunan Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism
 Hengyang: Preserving Culinary Heritage Further south, Hengyang occupies a significant place in Hunan's cultural landscape. Long regarded as an important regional center, the city has cultivated culinary traditions distinguished by craftsmanship, balance, and respect for established techniques. Its food culture reflects centuries of accumulated knowledge, demonstrating how local recipes and customs continue to evolve while remaining rooted in a strong sense of place.
 Rice noodles are a staple of Hunan’s rich gastronomic culture. © Hunan Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism
 Yiyang: A Reflection of Hunan's Rural Roots Beyond the provincial capital, Yiyang offers a quieter perspective on the region's culinary heritage. Surrounded by fertile agricultural land and waterways, the city is closely tied to the ingredients that have long sustained Hunan kitchens. Local food traditions emphasize straightforward preparations, preserved products, and the natural character of seasonal produce. Here, cuisine provides a window into the agricultural foundations upon which much of Hunan's gastronomic culture was built. 
 Stinky tofu and rice noodles, two emblematic dishes of Hunan’s local cuisine. © Hunan Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism
 A Province Best Understood Through Its Cuisine Together, Changsha, Yiyang, and Hengyang reveal different facets of Hunan's identity. Each city contributes its own voice to a broader culinary narrative shaped by geography, history, and local traditions. For visitors, the experience of Hunan is rarely confined to a list of landmarks. It is found in the rhythm of a morning market, the aroma rising from a neighborhood kitchen, the conversation shared around a crowded table, and the enduring connection between people and the food they prepare. In Hunan, cuisine is not simply something to be sampled. It is one of the most meaningful ways to understand the province itself.
