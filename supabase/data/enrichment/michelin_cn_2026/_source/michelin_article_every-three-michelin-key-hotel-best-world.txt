 It is the highest hotel distinction bestowed by Inspectors, the equivalent of the Three-Star for restaurants. Of our nearly 3,000 Key hotels in 2026, the Three Keys now number 155 — with 18 new additions joining in the latest reveal.Browse below and you’ll find that beyond top luxury and service, many Three-Key hotels provide their own distinct draws to a destination, with enough activities — adventure outings, private beaches and award-winning spas — to last for days.  Asia and OCEania
 35 top hotels across Australia, Mainland China, Vietnam, and more, including seven new additions in 2026.
More than any region in the world, Asia and Oceania’s Three-Key landscape is defined by sharp contrasts — ancient ritual alongside cutting-edge design, barefoot elegance with high luxury, imperial traditions newly reinvented for contemporary travelers. Here, hotels resist a single definition of excellence, in part because rarely do two places share the same climate, culture and philosophy. 
 The Taj Palace, a picturesque Three-Key hotel in India once seen in a James Bond film. © Taj Palace
Editor’s Picks: Highlights From the New Selection
Saffire Freycinet, Australia: One of two new Three-Key hotels in Oceania in 2026 (along with Southern Ocean Lodge), Saffire Freycient gleams just above the bush, under a stingray-shaped roof that houses 20 suites. Constructed carefully with local materials like oak and pine, each room faces toward the pink granite peaks of the Hazards. The isolated experience is a profoundly immersive one, with a restaurant serving hyper-local produce, a nearby oyster farm offering hands-on harvesting, and spa treatments that incorporate on native botanicals.Mandarin Oriental Qianmen, China: A finalist for this year’s MICHELIN Architecture and Design Award, Mandarin Oriental's second Beijing address is a notable departure from the ultra-modern, urban build of its sister hotel. In Qianmen, you’ll find something quite different; here, 42 houses wrap around individual courtyards, embracing one of Beijing's oldest neighborhoods with a renovation that preserves the traditional style, not to mention many of the materials, of the original, centuries-old buildings. 
Amanoi, Vietnam: Nui Chua National Park is a recognized UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, and it’s here on a hillside that Amanoi unfolds, offering sweeping views of white sand beaches and a turquoise Vinh Hy Bay. Rooms are luxurious to the nth degree — spa-like bathrooms, indoor-outdoor layouts with sun decks and, in some, private infinity pools — plus a lakeside spa and access to Vietnam’s wildest landscape.Browse by region or destination

 Europe
 A continent with a large percentage of the world's Three-Key hotels, Europe’s selection spans more than a dozen countries. Eight new hotels earn Three-Keys here in 2026.
Europe claims more Three-Keys than any other continent in the world, with over 80 in its region. This remarkable catalog spans restored castles in Italy, renowned dining destinations in Germany’s Black Forest, a handful of Swiss châteaux and cliffside retreats on Côte d’Azur. A stay at many a European Three-Key encourages travelers to time travel, blurring the boundary between past and present in a delightfully luxurious escape from the everyday. 
 Splendido Hotel in Portofino, one of four hotels in Italy to earn its first Three Keys in 2026. © Splendido, A Belmond Hotel
Editor’s Picks: Highlights From the Three-Key Selection
Château de la Chèvre d'Or, France: In a medieval village cut into the Côte d’Azur’s spectacular cliffside, Château de la Chèvre d'Or’s 45 rooms and suites blend exquisitely into the landscape. Elegance prevails in marble bathrooms and uncompromising views, but it's the public spaces that draw the discerning eye. Two pools perch dramatically on the rock face, offering a bird’s-eye-view of St. Jean-Cap-Ferrat; the top floor is reserved for Les Remparts, serving up fresh ceviche and stuffed courgette blossoms.Splendido, A Belmond Hotel, Italy: The Duke of Windsor was the first to sign the guest book in 1901 — and despite Splendido’s updates over the years, it remains a delightful journey back in time. First inhabited by 16th-century Benedictine monks, it remains one of Portofino’s finest hotels, with each of its recently redecorated rooms facing the Mediterranean Sea. A deep respect for Italy infuses the spaces, from the local materials to the hand-painted murals. Such was the attention to detail that even the color of the pool was painstakingly chosen to match the color of the sea.
Kulm Hotel St. Moritz, Switzerland: St. Moritz’s first luxury hotel dates back to 1856 and remains among the most lauded and high-end Alpine experiences in Europe. In four charmingly mismatched buildings are 150 rooms of varying categories, all with mountain or lake views. Meanwhile, waking hours call for adventures of all kinds: hitting the famous ski slopes, paragliding over the Alps or relaxing in the expansive wellness center.

 middle east and africa
 Of the nine Three-Key hotels throughout the Middle East and Africa in 2026, two are newcomers: with one each in South Africa and Saudi Arabia.
Africa and the Middle East’s Three-Key selections highlight the continent’s natural diversity and a rich cultural landscape that spans urban capitals, wide swaths of desert and a safari ecosystem like nothing else in the world. Like the very best hotels anywhere, both regions’ top spots belong wholly to their settings, but the architecture feels particularly grounded in the local when hotels use actual desert rock as their foundation.
 Desert Rock Resort in Saudi Arabia, winner of this year's MICHELIN Architecture & Design Award. © Desert Rock Resort
Editor’s Picks: Highlights From the New Selection
Delaire Graff Lodges & Spa, South Africa: The Cape Winelands are enough a draw in themselves. Add to that the extensive art collection and luxurious hospitality philosophy of this new Three-Key. Sixteen lodges — plus a four-bedroom Owner’s Villa — spread across the vineyards, each with private pools and, in the case of the villa, a butler and chef to boot. For all others, three restaurants range from a South African bistro to contemporary Japanese spot; wine tastings are, of course, available daily.Desert Rock Resort, Saudi Arabia: In a region that's no stranger to over-the-top design, Desert Rock Resort, the winner of the 2026 MICHELIN Architecture and Design Award and one of the region's latest Three-Keys, still manages to make eyes pop. Carved directly into the Hijaz mountains, 64 villas and suites are built of natural stone or on ridgelines, each with private pools and desert views. Floor-to-ceiling windows and low-impact lighting blurs the distinction between inside and out. A spa and stargazing terraces provide on-site diversions in an undeniably remote region that encourages desert exploration.
Atlantis the Royal, UAE: Winner of the inaugural MICHELIN Architecture and Design Award, the Atlantis the Royal is luxe even by Dubai standards. Here 795 ultraluxe rooms fill this reinvented version of a skyscraper, to go with 17 restaurants, including Dinner by Heston Blumenthal. The Royal Mansion penthouse, with its own infinity pool, is the peak.

 NORTH AMERICA
More than 20 hotels in the United States, Mexico, Canda and the Caribbean retained Three-Keys in 2026. 
Of the 21 Three-Key hotels in North America, 15 are in the United States, three in Mexico, two in Canada and one on the Caribbean island of St. Barts. Although no new Three-Key joins this list in 2026, the selection remains vast, a mix of the glitzy, design-first standard-bearers in major cities like New York and Los Angeles as well as hotels that trade on some of the world’s most miraculous natural settings, from one of the top glamping hotels in the world to the reimagined treehouses of the Mexican coastline.
 A mountain view from Sage Lodge, a Three-Key gem in the Western United States. © Sage Lodge
Editor’s Picks: Highlights From the Three-Key Selection
The Whitby Hotel, New York, USA: Cofounder and designer of the Firmdale Hotel group, Kit Kemp brings her typically ecstatic maximalist design to The Whitby, one of the aforementioned urban Three-Key standard-bearers on the continent. In Midtown Manhattan, this is among the world’s design gems, with every comfort, of course — including a movie theater.
Sage Lodge, Montana, USA: Here’s exactly what we mean by miraculous natural settings. One of several Three-Keys in the American West, Sage Lodge is set along the Yellowstone River, near enough to America’s most famous national park to offer its own curated tours of the grounds. Meanwhile, the lodge is a reward for venturing into the great outdoors, its luxury spa and farm-to-table dining from local ranches among its top attractions.
One&Only Mandarina, Mexico: An easy escape from the crowded coast of Puerto Vallarta for a stay in a luxe treehouse or a cliffside villa. Either way, expect butler service and your own plunge pool amid 80 acres of lush rainforest for a morning nature walks. Guests ride the resort buggy down to the white-sand beach for a private surf lesson, or else find the polo and equestrian club for rather unexpected athletics; a Greg Norman golf course is the finishing touch.

 South and CENTRAL America
 Across South and Central America, seven hotels hold our top distinction, including the region's latest addition in Costa Rica.
The astounding geography of South and Central America provides the inspiration for some of the world’s most stunning hotels. Brazil’s sublime Iguazú Falls, Chile’s surreal Patagonia and Costa Rica’s tropical Arenal Volcano may attract travelers, but their corresponding Three-Key properties give the experience another dimension — a mix of high luxury with high adventure.
 Freestanding lodges at Awasi Patagonia, one of two Three-Key hotels in Chilean Patagonia. © Awasi Patagonia
Editor’s Picks: Highlights From the Three-Key Selection
Awasi Patagonia, Chile: One of two Three-Key hotels in the bucket-list Torres del Paine National Park in Patagonia, Awasi makes its home out of just 14 villas, each a freestanding lodge that is rustic only on first glance. Guests choose from a roster of expert-guided excursions and return for locally sourced cuisine.
Las Casitas, a Belmond Hotel, Peru: You’re in the neighborhood of Machu Picchu, but Peru’s Colca Valley, in the heart of the Andes, has its own all-natural appeal. Over 20 acres, 20 casitas come with their own terraces and plunge pools, while guests fill days with hiking and birdwatching in this majestic setting.
Nekajui, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve, Costa Rica: Central America's latest Three-Key hotel is set on Peninsula Papagayo, some 1,400 acres of tropical forest and pristine beaches where you'll find some of the greatest hotels in the world. In 2026, Inspectors name Nekajui the very best of them: a central lodge orbited by a constellation of rooms, suites and villas — some of which are reached via a wooden suspension bridge that hangs across a ravine.
