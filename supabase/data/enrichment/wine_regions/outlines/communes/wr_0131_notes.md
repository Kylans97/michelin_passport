## Partials
- All 11 marked partial. The legal boundary is a parcel-level perimeter on cadastral maps; each commune is named only as the place where its map can be consulted. The perimeter is a narrow strip (the cahier: "Sa largeur oscille entre 300 et 400 mètres") on the Luxembourg side of the valley. No commune is left out, because every listed commune is in the same position and the commune list is the only list the texts give. If the app hatches partials, all of this region will be hatched.
- Rosport - Mompach is large and its perimeter covers only small areas (map labels: Born, Moersdorf, Givenich, Boursdorf, Herborn, Mompach, Rosport, Steinheim, Hinkel, Girst, Dickweiler, Osweiler). Mondorf-les-Bains and Waldbredimus maps are drawn at the smallest scale (small perimeters). Kept because the text lists them.
- Map labels (cadastral sections and localities shown on each 2009 map) are recorded in raw/lgx_rgd20090909n2.txt; they are not a section list, so not used.

## Appellation names checked
- Official: "Moselle Luxembourgeoise" (the AOP); "Crémant de Luxembourg" (a traditional term reserved to AOP wines, RGD 2016 art. 11; not a separate EU registration).
- RGD 2016 art. 8 allows the name of the section de commune, commune or canton; art. 9 reserves "Coteaux de" followed by a section, commune or canton name to wines from base yields of at most 75 hl/ha, hand picked; art. 10 reserves lieu-dit names (preceded by the section name), same limits, not for rosé or gris.
- Held back: "Côtes de Grevenmacher" and "Côtes de Remich". They appear as a marketing tier ("Les vins avec la mention d'origine « Côtes de » sont des vins harmonieux d'entrée de gamme") in the 2015 AOP leaflet (https://aop.public.lu/dam-assets/publications/20150429-depliant-aop-fr.pdf) but neither the 2020 cahier des charges nor the 2016 regulation names them. "Premier cru", "Grand premier cru" and "Vin classé" also appear in the 2020 cahier; not listed as appellations (traditional terms).
