# Canelones (Uruguay): drawn, 1 unit

## Official basis
- INAVI (Instituto Nacional de Vitivinicultura, public non-state body under Ley 15.903) defines Uruguay's wine regions by departamento on its regions page (https://www.inavi.com.uy/regiones/, saved raw/inavi_regiones.html, read 2026-11-21): METROPOLITANA = Canelones, Montevideo, San José. It describes Canelones as a region in its own right: "Es la región vitivinícola más grande del país, siendo responsable del 60% de la producción."
- Geographical indications: Decreto 283/993 (IMPO, https://www.impo.com.uy/bases/decretos/283-1993), Chapter IV: Art. 15 an IG, region of origin or DO on a label needs a certificate from the control body; Art. 16 INAVI keeps a Registro de Indicaciones Geográficas de Origen; Art. 17 an IG may be used only if the grapes were produced in the zone of that name. The decree lists no zones and defines no boundaries. The INV Argentina country report (2024) confirms VCP labels are registered with INAVI with a Certificado de Indicación Geográfica when an IG is named.
- A MIEM / Dirección Nacional de la Propiedad Industrial bulletin (2019, hosted at prosurproyecta.org; saved raw/prosur_ig.pdf) lists "Indicaciones Geográficas de Vinos reconocidas ante INAVI": Bella Unión, Atlántida, Canelón Chico, Canelones, Carmelo, Carpintería, Cerro Carmelo, Cerro Chapeu, Constancia, El Carmen, Garzón, José Ignacio, Juanicó, La Caballada, La Cruz, La Puebla. The list stops alphabetically at L and may be partial. No registered limits were found for any of them.
- So: the region is drawn as the whole departamento, following INAVI's department-based regions. It is NOT a drawing of the "Canelones" IG, whose limits are unpublished.
