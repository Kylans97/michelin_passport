# Guerrouane (AOG) — point only

## Official text
- Arrêté du ministre de l'agriculture et de la réforme agraire n° 869-75 du 15 août 1977 (BO n° 3388, 5 Oct 1977, p. 1091), Art. 1 6°: "Guerrouane : pour les vignobles du plateau de Meknès, situés au sud de la route de Fès-Meknès-Rabat et à l'ouest de la route de Meknès à El-Hajeb et à l'est de l'oued Beht." Consolidated text published by ONSSA: https://www.onssa.gov.ma/wp-content/uploads/Reglementation/R%C3%A9glementation%20Sectorielle/Produits%20Alimentaires/Boissons%20Alcoolis%C3%A9es/Vins/ARR.869-75.FR.pdf (saved: raw/onssa/ARR.869-75.FR.pdf). Also on WIPO Lex (MA008).
- Grapes allowed (Art. 2 b 1°, as completed by arrêté 1956-98, BO 4662, 4 Feb 1999): reds/rosés Cabernet franc, Cabernet sauvignon, Gamay, Grenache, Pinot, Syrah, Cinsault, Carignan, Alicante-Bouschet, Tempranillo, Merlot, Mourvèdre, Tannat, Cot, Petit Verdot; whites Clairette, Farane, Folle-Blanche, Grenache blanc, Maccabeo, Malvasia, Mersguera, Muscat blanc, Muscat d'Alexandrie, Pedro Ximénez, Sauvignon, Ugni blanc, El Biod (Zerhouni), Chardonnay, Niellucio. Minimum 12% natural alcohol, no enrichment, no irrigation without ministerial derogation.
- Nested AOC: Hauts Plateaux de Meknès (arrêté 3478-22, 16 Dec 2022, BO 7166, 2 Feb 2023, p. 297) lies inside the AOG Guerrouane: parts of the communes Aït Yazem, Mjat, Boufekrane, El Hajeb, Ifrane, Azrou, between road P7017 (La Kantina) to the west, N13 Meknès-El Hajeb then R707 to Ifrane to the east, and the Meknès-Fès motorway to the north.

## Units
None. The AOG is defined by roads and a river, not by administrative units. No shape is drawn: point only.

## Point
33.73148, -5.5791 — GeoNames 8542600 "Ait Yaazem" (P.PPL), from the GeoNames MA dump downloaded 2026-11-20 (raw/geonames/MA.txt). Why: arrêté 3478-22 names Aït Yazem as a commune inside the AOG Guerrouane, and the village satisfies the 1977 description (south of Meknès, west of the Meknès-El Hajeb road). It stands for the AOG as a whole, not a vineyard.

## Importance
- Wine-Searcher groups Guerrouane with Beni M'Tir, Saïss and Zerhoun in the Meknès/Fès region (https://www.wine-searcher.com/regions-morocco, read through search snippets: the page returns 403 to direct fetch).
- Jancis Robinson's tasting database labels current wines "Guerrouane" (Dom de la Zouina Volubilia Gris and Epicuria Chardonnay; Les Deux Domaines, Domaine Jirry Chardonnay 2023): https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/dom-de-la-zouina-volubilia-gris-2007-guerrouane, https://www.jancisrobinson.com/tastings/290009, https://www.jancisrobinson.com/tastings/302604.
- Oxford Companion to Wine, 3rd ed. (2006), "Morocco", pp. 453-454 (read via its citation in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_wine, not the book): Meknès/Fès is one of five regions; Guerrouane is among its AOGs.

## Style
- Summary (draft, 2 sources): Vineyards on the Meknès plateau at the foot of the Middle Atlas; altitude moderates a hot, dry climate. Reds, rosés and especially pale vin gris; Moroccan reds are mostly from Mediterranean grapes. Sources: Jancis Robinson (Meknès ~700 m next to the Middle Atlas; vin gris "a Moroccan speciality", Volubilia Gris review), Wine-Searcher Morocco page (Meknès "midway between" Middle Atlas and Atlantic, sheltered; producer altitude 580-700 m).
- Soils: clay-limestone is stated only by trade/retail pages (thewineilove.com, winewithseth.com) for Zouina/Meknès: held back.
- Main grapes: no region-specific ranking found in two sources: held back. National picture (OCW via Wikipedia): Cinsault and Carignan traditional, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah rising (~15% together, 2005 data).
- Typical red body/acidity/tannin: no source describes a Guerrouane red's structure: held back.
- Pairing: none found: held back.
- Label: arrêté 869-75 Art. 6 — the label shows the geographical name "Guerrouane" with "appellation d'origine garantie" in very apparent characters directly under it; "Domaine", "Château", "Coteau", "Côte" etc. are allowed only on AOG/AOC wine from a delimited estate existing at least 5 years.
