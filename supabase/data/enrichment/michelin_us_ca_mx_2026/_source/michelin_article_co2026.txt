        On September 14, 2026, the Selection for the MICHELIN Guide Colorado was revealed, spotlighting a total of 10 Starred restaurants. Below, you can delve deeper into the culinary trends that are defining the state's gastronomy right now or simply browse every single Star. 
        New Cities Join the SelectionIn addition to Aspen, Avon, Beaver Creek, Boulder, Denver and Vail, the 2026 Selection includes four new cities, pushing the radius of MICHELIN Guide restaurants in Colorado further to the southwest. Those locations include two major, rapidly growing cities along the Front Range, Aurora (Annette, Dân Dã) and Colorado Springs (Four by Brother Luck, The Penrose Room), plus the popular ski towns of Breckenridge (Radicato, Restaurant Olivia, Rootstalk) and Telluride (The National). 
                The National brings warm and cozy food to the ski town of Telluride; Chef Eduardo Valle Lobo emphasizes seafood and Iberian ingredients like jamón and pork at Casa Juani. © The National | © Casey Wilson/Casa Juani
        Diverse Dining Delights in a Land-Locked StateAlong with the seven dining spots in these added cities, the expanded selection boasts a grand total of 15 new restaurants, including one new Star, three Bib Gourmand and 11 recommended. Inspectors noted that these inscriptions show a striking diversity in cuisines, including Basque/Spanish, Italian, Mexican, Southern, Taiwanese and Vietnamese, which they found impressive considering how the state is land locked. The only new Star, Milpero in Denver, features a multicourse Mexican tasting menu in which Chef Johnny Curiel has built entirely around corn. The restaurant grows its own heirloom, ancestral Mexican corn just southeast of Boulder, while its kitchen team nixtamalizes and grinds it into masa in-house.  Southern is a new cuisine for Colorado, and Rougarou in Denver has made it into a true family affair. Owned by husband-and-wife team Mclain Hedges and Mary Allison Wright, with the kitchen helmed by Executive Chef John David Wright (Mary’s brother), the funky, New Orleans-inspired spot specializes in casual Southern comfort food with creative, contemporary twists. Think boiled peanuts and pimento cheese along with Creole tofu, hot and sour catfish and koji cured pork steak.  Inspectors also noted that Spanish/Basque food is trending in Colorado, but that chefs are approaching the cuisine in different ways. At Casa Juani in Boulder, Chef Eduardo Valle Lobo reinterprets traditional Spanish dishes from his upbringing in Madrid with contemporary technique, while emphasizing seafood and Iberian ingredients like jamón and pork. In Denver, Heretík also has a seafood-focused menu, but Chef/owner Theo Adley blends a Basque-leaning, coastal Spanish approach with traditional French cooking, with dishes such as pintxos, oysters and a signature French-style rotisserie chicken. 
                Vietnamese food at Dân Dã; The Penrose Room is the place to eat and be seen. © Paul Camagong/Dân Dã | © The Broadmoor/The Penrose Room
        Colorado-Grown Ingredients ShineAccording to Inspectors, locally grown Colorado ingredients are being celebrated in casual and elevated settings alike, from the heirloom corn grown on a local farm for the masa program at Milpero to Fitch Ranch beef served at Rootstalk, a contemporary spot in new-to-the Selection Breckinridge. Other items that have appeared on menus include wild mushrooms, such as at Bosq in Aspen, plus lamb, river trout, duck and berries.  Additionally, three restaurants — including Boulder’s Bramble & Hare and Brutø, and The Wolf's Tailor in Denver — are part of MICHELIN’s new Mindful Voices series, which highlights chefs who are pioneering inspiring initiatives in gastronomy. Many of these chefs also focus on local ingredient sourcing. 
                Rootstalk gets its beef from Fitch Ranch; Four by Brother Luck is the newest addition to Colorado Springs. © Yvonne Albinowski/Rootstalk | © Ashley Brown/Four by Brother Luck
        A Historic Icon Continues to Hit the SpotOpened on New Year’s Eve in 1961 on the top floor of the South Tower in Colorado Spring’s legendary Broadmoor hotel, The Penrose Room has joined the Guide as a recommended restaurant after its massive renovation, which was completed in the fall of 2025. The storied American fine dining spot, where jackets are still required for men, has also maintained its air of occasion by taking home the 2026 award for Best Service.  Three other restaurants received a special award, including Four by Brother Luck for Best Cocktail, Heretík for Best Sommelier and Radicato for Best Young Chef.
