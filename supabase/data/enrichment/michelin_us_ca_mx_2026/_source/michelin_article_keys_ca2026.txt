        After a global reveal on Sept. 18, 2026 that announced hundreds of new Keys around the world, our Inspectors’ list of the very best hotels in Canada has been updated with brand new additions and upgrades. Here, six new One-Key hotels and one new Two-Key join the selection, while the country’s two Three-Keys retain their top MICHELIN honors.  Like the MICHELIN Stars for restaurants, the MICHELIN Keys distinguish the most outstanding hotels in our selection.         City Breaks: Food, Culture and Urban Life
Amid Montreal’s mix of quaint cobblestone streets and ultra-modern architecture is a well-established culinary scene. At a couple of new One Keys, you need not leave the premises for MICHELIN-recommended dining: The Ritz-Carlton Montreal is home to Maison Boulud, while at Four Seasons, celebrity chef Marcus Samuelsson heads Marcus’ eclectic menu.
For those with another sort of travel appetite, Quebec City’s Fairmont Le Château Frontenac retains its Key, and puts guests squarely within a tourist attraction — the Canadian railway hotel, built to resemble a castle and soaring above the St. Lawrence River bluff, is a landmark in its own right. In Toronto, Ace Hotel retains its Key as well, with vintage-inspired architecture and a Mediterranean menu at Adler. Its Garment District setting places guests near art galleries and baseball games at Rogers Center. 
Meanwhile, Kingston makes a notable splash this year — the waterfront university city sports a pair of new One-Keys: Belvedere Hotel, a 19th-century chateau turned luxury boutique steps from Lake Ontario, and the Smith Hotel, a converted stone church featuring loft apartments with exposed brick and a ground floor co-working space.
                The One-Key Post Hotel & Spa in Alberta, ideal in summer or ski season. © Post Hotel & Spa
        Mountain Scenery: Ski and Hiking Destinations
In one of the world’s most scenic countries, dominated by the Rockies and sparkling glacial lakes, Key hotels are equally picturesque settings. Some of the most gorgeous include a renovated WWII-era riverside ski lodge (Post Hotel & Spa) in Lake Louise, Mont Tremblant’s 30-suite boutique Hotel Quintessence, and Nita Lake Lodge, a modern-rustic ski resort at the base of Whistler Mountain — open all year. 
Worth a mention too is Canada’s “Castle in the Rockies,” sitting pretty in the heart of Banff National Park. From the outside, Fairmont Banff Springs indeed mirrors a royal residence, with rooms that are comfortably modern and well-equipped for a night’s sleep. You’ll need it for days of hiking, swimming, skiing and paddling. Add to that an expansive spa and mountain-ringed golf course.
                Clayoquot Wilderness Lodge, the rare glamping hotel in the world with Three Keys. © Clayoquot Wilderness Lodge
        Rural and Wilderness Escapes + Canada's Pair of Three-Keys 
The word “rural” is relative — if the country roads and rocky shores of Prince Edward County suffice, a new One-Key, Merrill House, is equal parts eclectic (one of 14 individually designed rooms features a chair on the ceiling) and elegant, with linens that are finely French, and robes satin. It joins the One-Key Royal Hotel, just up Main Street in the same lovely little town of Picton, a family-run and restored brick beauty with all the trappings of a classic guesthouse, plus a spa and farm-to-table restaurant that emphasizes local wines. 
At the edge of Vancouver Island, between a lush forest and wild coast, Wickaninnish Inn (One Key) sits atop rugged bedrock, where Pacific views unfold from all 75 guest rooms’ windows. For such a remote atmosphere, it’s hard to believe you’re a six-minute drive from Tofino’s lively town center. Forty-five minutes by boat — or, should you prefer, seaplane — and you’ll arrive at Clayoquot Wilderness Lodge, where Three-Key glamping tents sit amidst the picture-perfect, UNESCO-designated Clayoquot Sound Biosphere Reserve. 
On the Atlantic side, Fogo Island Inn is Canada’s other Three-Key hotel, with an ultra-modern design that rightly emphasizes floor-to-ceiling windows. An extremely remote location on a tiny and sparsely populated island off the coast of Newfoundland adds to its allure.
