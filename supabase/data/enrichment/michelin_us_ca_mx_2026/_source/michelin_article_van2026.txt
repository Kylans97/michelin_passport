        On October 6, 2026, the Selection for the MICHELIN Guide Vancouver was revealed, spotlighting a total of 11 Starred restaurants. Below, you can delve deeper into the culinary trends that are defining the Canadian city's gastronomy right now or simply browse every single Star. 
                AnnaLena has been promoted from One to Two Stars. © Allison Kuhl/AnnaLena
        Restaurants Show Global Excellence The stars are shining a little brighter this year for AnnaLena in Kitsilano. Known for its creative, modern Canadian fare with global influences, the stylish but playful tasting-menu restaurant run by Chef Michael Robbins has been promoted from One to Two Stars.  Many Bib Gourmand and MICHELIN-recommended restaurants that are new to the Selection highlight cuisines that pair international techniques and concepts with chefs' personal, cultural backgrounds. Creative Indian food is just one of the cuisines Inspectors are seeing as part of Vancouver's broader trend towards global flavors.  With newly awarded Bib Gourmand status, Mount Pleasant’s Restaurant Kavita, meaning "poetry," takes modern Indian cuisine back to the nostalgic realm. Drawing on Chef Tushar Tondvalkar's upbringing in Mumbai and the seaside town of Malvan, dishes draw on his memories of eating street food and cooking over fire with his family's matriarchs. The Ammakase tasting menu, a mashup of the Indian word for "mother" (amma) and Japanese omakase, invites diners into the world of Indian flavors and heritage, one small course at a time. Kavita is also this year's recipient of Vancouver's special award for Best Service.  Peya, located in East Vancouver, is a celebration of French and Indian traditions, whereby Chef Ashwani Dabas applies French culinary techniques to the Indian dishes he grew up with in New Delhi. Menu items include fig and goat cheese samosa with mint chutney and crème fraiche, Dungeness crab rasam (a tangy tamarind soup) and malai chicken with Boursin sauce — all followed by chai tea crème brulée and coconut macaroons. Peya’s bar manager, Bar Manager Chris Enns, has also been given this year's Best Cocktail Award for Vancouver.  With distinctive bold but balanced flavors on display, Inspectors found that Jay Nok Modern Thai in Olympic Village demonstrates care and a sense of authenticity in its of Thai cuisine. Here, even popular plates like pad Thai and khao soi (an egg noodle curry soup) are given an extra bit of flair with the likes of grilled pork jowl and bone marrow.  Featuring bold, modern Korean cuisine, Jeju in Mount Pleasant is a family affair by husband-and-wife team Dylan and Sung Kim, who run the back of the house and front of the house, respectively. Dylan’s mother, Myungseok (also known as Julie), cooks in the kitchen as well. While the menu is built around Korean flavors reminiscent of Dylan and Sung's childhood memories in South Korea, refined techniques and ingredients are applied to dishes such as gochujang glazed fried chicken, bulgogi with truffle and sliced rib eye, and soondubu, a spicy silken tofu stew with prawn and squid. 
                Peya applies French culinary techniques to homey Indian dishes. Jeju features bold, modern Korean cuisine. © Peya | © Ian Harland/Jeju
        Menus Showcase Vancouver Ingredients Vancouver’s food culture is heavily built around the local producers and seasonality of the area’s diverse geography, which includes waterways, mountains, forests and fertile farmland. More and more chefs are using the location as a culinary advantage and are making the seasons and local ingredients a large part of their restaurant's identity.  Another new inscription, Niwa, is Japanese inspired when it comes to techniques but builds its menu around Pacific Northwest seasonality and Chef/owner Darren Gee's personal style. With a name that means "garden" in Japanese, this East Vancouver farm-to-table establishment offers à la carte and omakase menus that change frequently with locally sourced ingredients like fava beans roasted in the shell, Wagyu zabuton steak and three-week dry aged pork.  With its prime location near the Pacific Ocean, Vancouver is a fishmonger's paradise. As such, chefs like Alex Guido of Fanny Bay Oyster Bar put local seafood at the center of the plate. With its "tide to table" concept, the Downtown Vancouver spot shortens the distance it takes for seafood to enter the kitchen by running its own shellfish hatchery and farm. In the dining room, the seasonal oyster selection is served with lemon, horseradish and mignonette, along with signature sauces such as mango habanero and smoky chipotle. The raw bar, Sea'cuterie Board and à la carte items all feature seafood.  Restaurants like Nero Tondo in East Vancouver use as many British Colombian ingredients as possible, having close relationships with the purveyors that supply them. Inspectors highlighted dishes like kohlrabi mixed with caramelized honey, makrut lime nahm jim (Thai dipping sauce) and wildflower honey gelato. The Acorn in Mount Pleasant forages many of its ingredients — such as wild mushrooms, nettles, pinecones and edible flowers — from within a 100-kilometer (about 62-mile) radius.
