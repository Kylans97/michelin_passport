        On October 8, 2026, the Selection for the MICHELIN Guide Texas was revealed, spotlighting a total of 19 Starred restaurants. Two restaurants, Goldee's Bar-B•Q in Fort Worth and Fabrik, a vegan spot in Austin, were promoted to One Star, while French Japanese Kappo Kappo, also in Austin, debuted in the MICHELIN Guide with One Star.Below, you can delve deeper into the culinary trends that are shaping the state's gastronomy right now or simply browse every single Star.  
                Goldee's Bar-B•Q in Fort Worth has been promoted from Bib Gourmand to One Star. © Will Milne/ Goldee's Bar-B•Q
        Barbecue Restaurants Continue to Succeed  Pitmasters are facing serious headwinds due to the rising cost of beef in the U.S., yet Inspectors note that many are still able to strive for excellence at their barbecue restaurants.  Goldee's Bar-B•Q in Fort Worth shines especially bright as it has been promoted from Bib Gourmand status to One Star. Founded by a group of friends in 2019 and opened in 2020, the no-frills Texas barbecue joint usually has a long queue out the door, although patrons don’t mind waiting for the beef and pork ribs, jalapeño-cheddar sausage and signature brisket, which is cooked low and slow using local post oak.  Levant BBQ, a new MICHELIN-recommended restaurant in Houston, blends Central Texas barbecue with Levantine flavors. Here, classic smoked meats like brisket and ribs live side-by-side with items such as lamb chops, fasolia (a Middle Eastern bean dish) and hummus. With the brisket shawarma, the two cuisines come together seamlessly in the same dish.  
                At Kappo Kappo, brothers Haru and Gohei Kishi bring together their Japanese background and experience living in France with an intimate chef-counter omakase and French cooking techniques. © Proper Hotel/Kappo Kappo
        Personal, Chef-Driven Projects Drive Menus  Many chefs in Texas are building restaurants and dining experiences around a singular creative vision, culinary philosophy or the chef's personal heritage.  In Dallas, Chef Olivia López has created Olōyō with a strong identity around heirloom Mexican corn, masa and the cooking of Colima, a state on Mexico’s Pacific coast. While the menu is highly seasonal, dishes like tamales, huaraches (thick corn tortillas with toppings) and sopes (masa-based dough with pinched-up rims) often appear.   At new Bib Gourmand Xolo, Chef Javier Becerra brings Mexico City to Houston though creative cocktails, bar snacks and small plates, including the “Guava Matcha Tini,” tuna tostada and triple-cooked octopus with mole blanco.  The husband-and-wife team behind Houston's Street to Kitchen, Chef Benchawan "G" Jabthong and Graham Painter, has recently opened an ambitious new contemporary restaurant next door called Jantra. This intimate, eight-seat spot departs from the traditional Thai offerings of its Bib Gourmand sister restaurant by featuring a tasting-menu that combines Thai flavors, global ingredients and Chef G’s childhood memories and travels though Thailand, Myanmar and the U.S.   At Kappo Kappo, brothers Haru and Gohei Kishi bring together their Japanese background and experience living in France to a format that's uniquely theirs: an intimate chef-counter omakase with French cooking techniques, featuring recurring items like toro tartare and Wagyu paired with classic French sauces.  
                Farm-to-table is on the menu at Fabrik. © Je Wallerstein/Fabrik
        Texas Farm-to-Table is on the MenuRelationships with Texas farmers, ranchers and millers are increasingly becoming integral to restaurants in the state. According to Inspectors, Harvest at the Masonic in McKinney is the most complete example for its sourcing of produce from Water Boy Farms, which is directed by Executive Chef and Master Gardener Andrea Shackelford. Other prime farm-to-table restaurants include Birdie's, One-Star Barley Swine (winner of the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Texas Service Award) and new One-Star Fabrik in Austin, as well as Lovers Pizzeria, a Selection newcomer in San Antonio.
