        On June 24, 2026, the latest Selection for the MICHELIN Guide California was revealed, spotlighting a total of 83 Starred restaurants. Below, you can delve deeper into the culinary trends that are defining California's gastronomy right now or simply browse every single Star, organized by number of Stars, then alphabetically by town/city. The two newcomers to the MICHELIN-Starred Selection for 2026 appear as "new," so you can easily identify the state’s newest sought-after restaurants.
        California Makes History With the First Three-Starred Mexican RestaurantSan Francisco-based Californios is now the world's first Mexican restaurant to earn Three MICHELIN Stars. We sat down with our MICHELIN Guide Inspectors to learn more about what it takes to earn Three Stars and how Californios has impressed them over the years, earning One, Two and now Three Stars.         California Raises the Bar in the U.S. for Top-Tier DiningIt is yet another banner year for California, with two new Three Stars, one new Two Star and eight new One Stars, but it's not just about awards. California continues to impress our Inspectors with its diversity of cuisines and culinary approaches at all levels. While Californios sets a standard for contemporary Mexican food globally, Maria Isabel, a Recommended restaurant also in San Francisco, shows a more down-to-earth expression of the cuisine that is compelling in its own right. Enclos represents a happy marriage between California’s classic wine country cuisine with a modern edge, embodying the ascendency of Sonoma as a serious culinary destination. Naides in San Francisco is an exciting new benchmark for ambitious, chef-driven Filipino cuisine (and is one of only a few Starred Filipino restaurants), while Kato celebrates Chef Jonathan Yao’s family roots in Taiwan and the diaspora culture of Los Angeles' San Gabriel Valley.  
                Dish presented in a molcajete and an elevated fish taco at Californios. © Val Cantu/Californios
        Nonalcoholic Pairings Are on the Rise California is well known for its wine country, but the Golden State's restaurants are responding to guests' growing desire for nonalcoholic beverages with creative and thoughtful pairings. Exemplary programs at restaurants like Naides, Seline and Kato draw upon house ferments, foraged ingredients and the use of kitchen scraps for inventive drinks. 
                Basil clams dish and Pimm’s Cup over shaved ice. © Colleen O'Brien /Kato
        California's Ingredients Continue to Impress It may not be news that California produces top-quality ingredients, but they keep getting better and better. Restaurants sourcing locally are blessed with a bevy of the best, from Wolfe Ranch quail to Masami Cattle Ranch American Wagyu. Local sourcing is leveled up in the state, where restaurants like Addison in San Diego serve strawberries from Harry's Berries, while Monte's in Santa Barbara and Restaurant Ki in Los Angeles source their fruit from Rincon Hill Farm. Seafood options like line-caught black cod and sustainably farmed abalone, Baja bluefin (often seen in San Diego) and Tomales Bay Oysters are seen far and wide. It's a bounty unlike any other state in the country.
