        On September 22, 2026, the Selection for the MICHELIN Guide Toronto & Region was revealed, spotlighting a total of 15 Starred restaurants. Below, you can delve deeper into the culinary trends that are defining the city and surrounding region's gastronomy right now or simply browse every single Star. 
                © Scott Usheroff/Restaurant 20 Victoria | © Enigma Yorkville/Enigma Yorkville
        Restaurants With New Chefs Keep StarsWhile no new restaurants have been awarded Stars for 2026, two restaurants have retained their Stars despite changes to the chefs helming their kitchens.At One Star Enigma Yorkville, a contemporary fine dining spot that focuses on classic European cuisine with a modern Canadian ethos, Chef Zachary Barnes has taken the place of founding Executive Chef Quinton Bennett, who stepped down at the end of January in 2026. Barnes, who has experience working at Restaurant Gordon Ramsay in London, Daniel in New York and Alo in Toronto, has transitioned Bennett’s complex, avant-garde approach into one that emphasizes simplicity, letting the ingredients shine above all else. One course on Barnes’ menu — a refined take on chicken ballotine, which is stuffed with lobster, rolled into a cylinder and dressed in an Arabica coffee-infused lobster sauce — harkens back to 19th-century France. Barnes has also flipped the script on the Enigma Signature Tree: the interactive display of delights that once closed the show with dessert petit fours now stands in as a savory opening act of canapés, such as short rib croquettes and salmon mille-feuille.  Restaurant 20 Victoria has said goodbye to Chef Julie Hyde, who led the kitchen through the end of 2025, and welcomed Rafael Covarrubias, a Mexican Canadian chef hailing from the former Hexagon Restaurant in Oakville, Ontario. Drawing on his Mexican heritage, Covarrubias has steered the menu away from its elegantly restrained European dishes towards bolder, contemporary flavors. Dishes enjoyed by Inspectors include tamale with salsa roja, and snow crab and scallop with mole verde. 
                © Jeremy Austin/LORE | © Jose Jacob/Revé
        Great Food Beyond Toronto ProperToronto might be a Canadian dining hotspot, but Inspectors have continued to see more culinary ambition in areas beyond the city limits.  The team at One-Starred The Pine in Creemore, located 130 kilometers (about 80 miles) north of Toronto, has recently opened LORE just a 25-minute drive up the road in Collingwood. While The Pine features modern Chinese and East Asian cuisine in a fine-dining space, LORE pivots towards seafood-forward Canadian in a casual, neighborhood setting. Two multicourse menus of varying depth, called Provenance and Abundance, highlight whimsical dishes like scallop nuggets, a nostalgic take on a crispy fried fast-food item, and “The Holiday Shrimp Ring,” an elegant single-bite course made to evoke the classic party platter of cold shrimp around a tub of cocktail sauce.  South of the city along the western curve of Lake Ontario, the Niagara region is gaining recognition from the Guide. One of the most ambitious openings in the area is Revé in the charming, historic Old Town of Niagara-on-the-Lake. Here, Chef Adriano Cappuzzello employs a “farm-to-fire" philosophy centered around cooking hyper-local ingredients on an open-flame hearth. Dishes include Lake Erie Pickerel, which is dressed with red beets, Swiss chard and fish velouté, while the local Beverly Creek Farm beef bavette is accompanied by crespelle di potato (Italian fried dough fritters with potato) and Bordelaise sauce.  Delighting diners and Inspectors alike in nearby St. Catharines are two bistros: Les Incompetents, offering a playful French-inspired menu courtesy of Chef Zach Smith and the team behind Fat Rabbit, and oddBird, which specializes in gourmet comfort foods like buttermilk fried chicken and house ground burgers.   RPM Bakehouse in Jordan Village takes the cake, literally. The cafe and bakery sources produce from Two-Star Restaurant Pearl Morissette’s 1.5-acre farm and garden, along with ingredients like heritage grains from other local farmers and millers, transforming them into casual breakfast and lunch bites such as sandwiches, salads, dips, pastries and breads. There is also cake, of course, in the form of rotating pound cakes and seasonal coffee and fruit cakes. 
                © Nazar Pashchynskyi/Antylia | © Paula Wilson/PUNCH
        Toronto Shines With Diverse CuisinesRounding out the 2026 Selection is a diverse range of cuisines at restaurants that have been given the Bib Gourmand and recommended nod. Cuisines represented in the new guide entries include Barbecue (Golden Horseshoe), Indian (PUNCH), Italian (Bar ARDO), Latin American (Antylia), Korean (156 ONEFIVESIX and Takja) and Vietnamese (Pho Ngoc Yen and The Lunch Lady of Saigon).Special awards have been given to 156 ONEFIVESIX for Best Cocktail program, LORE for Best Service, Restaurant 20 Victoria for Best Sommelier and Golden Horseshow Barbecue for Best Young Chef.
