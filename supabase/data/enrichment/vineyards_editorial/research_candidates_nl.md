# Dutch wine estates: candidate shortlist (Part 2)

Researched 2026-11-20. This is a shortlist for a product-owner decision, **not verified records**. Nothing here has had the full field-by-field treatment that Apostelhoeve got.
Each estate's address, website and visitor details come from the estate's own site, fetched directly. Recognition is cited per source. Order is by **strength of the evidence**, not by taste.

## Evidence sources used (and how much weight each carries)

1. **Rijks wine list, July 2026.** Rijks (Amsterdam) holds 1 MICHELIN star (our own catalogue, `rest_0093`, https://guide.michelin.com/nl/nl/north-holland/amsterdam/restaurant/rijks). Its wine list has a page titled "Nederlandse wijndomeinen op deze wijnkaart" with a map that names seven estates: de Kleine Schorre, Dassemus, Wijngoed Thorn, Domein de Wijngaardsberg, De Apostelhoeve, Domein Steenberg and Wijnhoeve de Colonjes. Wines from Apostelhoeve, Wijngaardsberg and Thorn are listed by name with prices. Source: https://www.rijksrestaurant.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/RIJKS_WINEMENU_2026-1.pdf (text extracted locally: `raw/rijks_2026_07.txt`). This is the strongest gastronomy signal I found. It is one restaurant, though.
2. **Wijnkeuring van de Lage Landen 2025.** The annual competition for Dutch and Belgian wines, run by Stichting Wijninstituut Nederland. Wines are judged blind by the Weinbauamt in Neustadt alongside the German Landesprämierung, and results were announced on 10 Sep 2025. There were 54 estates and 230 wines. Source: https://media-new.imu.nl/storage/domeinbergen.nl/31034/uitslag-wijnkeuring-van-de-lage-landen-2025.pdf (text: `raw/wllo2025.txt`). Caveat: only estates that enter can win. **None of Apostelhoeve, Thorn, Kleine Schorre or Wijngaardsberg appears in the 2025 results at all.** That means they did not enter; it says nothing about their quality.
3. **DutchNews.nl, "Wine watch: a winter celebration of Dutch wines"** by Vicky Hampton, 1 Dec 2025. A wine column that recommends specific estates. Source: https://www.dutchnews.nl/2025/12/wine-watch-a-winter-celebration-of-dutch-wines
4. **Oxford Companion to Wine, "Netherlands" entry** (Jancis Robinson, ed.), as hosted on ckbk. It returned **HTTP 403** to me, so I saw it only through search-result excerpts: Kleine Schorre is "one of the best Dutch producers", and St. Martinus is the biggest vineyard "so far" at 11 ha. Source: https://app.ckbk.com/reference/wine05383c14s001e033 — **someone should open this page before quoting it.**
5. **Kassa (BNNVARA) blind test of Dutch white wine**, Sept 2020 (consumer TV, panel included sommelier Ronnie Brouwer). Source: https://www.bnnvara.nl/kassa/artikelen/test-witte-wijn-van-nederlandse-bodem (seen through a search excerpt only, not fetched).

I could not use:
- **Perswijn**: scores are behind the magazine paywall. Issue #5 2026 has a BOB Mergelland tasting I could not read.
- **Jancis Robinson producer pages**: 403.
- **Decanter World Wine Awards**: no Dutch results surfaced in search.
- A national "Nederlandse Wijnwedstrijd" under that name: I did not find one. The national competition I found is the Wijnkeuring van de Lage Landen.

## Checked, not shortlisted (on the evidence, not on merit)

- **Hoeve Nekum**, Maastricht (Nekummerweg 31, 6212 NK; https://www.hoevenekum.nl; BOB Mergelland stated by the estate; Saturday shop and excursions by phone). The only recognition I found is the estate's own: "beste wijngoed van Limburg" in 2003 (it does not say who awarded it), and the wine for the 2 Feb 2002 royal wedding (60 bottles of Riesling 1999). It is not on the Rijks list, not in the WLL 2025 results, and not in the DutchNews column. Hold it until there is independent evidence. It is a good *visitable* Maastricht estate, but there is no current citable acclaim.
- Not checked in depth, but named by one source each: **Wijngoed Wolf** (Brabant, biodynamic; DutchNews Dec 2025), **Wijngaard De Frysling** (Friesland; WLL 2025 gold plus DutchNews), **Wijngaard Hof van Twente** (WLL 2025, 4 golds), **Keulenhof** (WLL 2025, 4 golds), **Landgoed Overst** (Voerendaal; owner named Dutch Winemaker of the Year 2010 per a Jancis Robinson article, seen as an excerpt only). Any of these could be "the other really nice one" if the product owner recognises the name.

## Near-misses
- Rijks prints "Domein de Wijngaardsberg, **Maastricht**". The estate's own visiting address is **Ulestraten** (municipality Meerssen). Use the estate's address.
- Rijks prints "Wijngaard Thorn" in one place and "Wijngoed Thorn" in another. The estate writes **Wijngoed Thorn**. `wijngoedthorn.nl` (no hyphen) is a dead STRATO parking domain; the real site is `wijngoed-thorn.nl`.
- `wijngaardsberg.nl` is a reserved/parked domain. The real site is `dewijngaardsberg.nl`.
- International Wine Challenge medals for "The King's Thorn" Pinot Gris belong to a New Zealand wine. They are **not** Wijngoed Thorn's.
- For the product owner's "another really nice one": the estates with the strongest *gastronomy* signal that are also open to visitors are **Kleine Schorre** and **Thorn** (Thorn has a tasting shop only, no tours). **Wijngaardsberg** is just as strong on the evidence but takes no visitors.
