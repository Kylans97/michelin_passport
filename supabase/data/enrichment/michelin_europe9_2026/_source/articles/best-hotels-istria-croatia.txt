Inside 5 of the Best Hotels in Istria, Croatia’s Beloved Gastronomy Region
Inland and along the water, five distinct ways to experience the best of Istria’s historic towns and renowned cuisine.
                            Editor's Pick
                            Hotels
                            Travel
                            Croatia
                            Michelin Guide
Share
It may be at the northern end of the country’s Adriatic coast, but Croatia’s region of Istria is as much about what lies inland as the scene along the water. While the coast hums to a rhythm of fishing villages, wooded coves and historic seaside towns, the region inland is a landscape of medieval hilltop towns, olive groves and vineyards.  
On both ends of its geography, Istria thrives on food and wine — from Istrian truffles and olive oil to traditional local pasta and wine. All may be found near the five excellent hotels below, each selected by MICHELIN Guide Inspectors, each a distinct way to experience this varied corner of Croatia.

Meneghetti Wine Hotel and Winery
  What it's all about: A One-Key vineyard estate where wine, gastronomy and the surrounding Istrian landscape are central to the stay.
Set among vineyards and centuries-old olive groves near the historic town of Bale, Meneghetti Wine Hotel & Winery offers accommodations that range from intimate rooms to spacious private villas with their own pools. But at the heart of the estate is undeniably the winery, which produces its own varietals to complement the simple, Mediterranean-inspired dishes at the MICHELIN-selected restaurant, Meneghetti.
The setting is deliberately peaceful, with the stone buildings, gardens, and rows of vines here to create a sense of separation from the busier coastal resorts. Wine tastings offer an opportunity to explore the local vintages as well as international varieties and the broader character of Istrian viticulture. During harvest season, guests can take this experience a step further, actually joining the grape harvest itself and the atmosphere of the season alongside the estate’s dedicated team.
The serene grounds of San Canzian. © San Canzian
San Canzian Hotel & Residences
  What it’s all about: A One-Key retreat built around an historic Istrian village, where local heritage, design, food and wine are all major highlights.
A quiet, contemporary retreat that feels firmly rooted in the Istrian countryside, San Canzian is set in the hills near the small town of Buje, its spaces set amidst a restored medieval village where traditional stone architecture meets contemporary interiors, art and design. Expect plenty of natural stone and wood, locally crafted pieces, and plenty of facilitated local experiences – among them, tours of local wineries, private visits to basilicas and Porsche rentals for rolling through the countryside. Look no further than Giannina, the goat the hotel adopted on a local family farm, for evidence of the hotel’s commitment to its region.
Meanwhile, at 
Luciano
, the hotel's MICHELIN-selected restaurant, Chef Pavo Klarić prioritizes seasonal ingredients and local suppliers. During September, the bar joins in with Istrian Negroni Month, presenting seven signature Negroni creations.
A distinctive build catches the eye. © Lone Hotel
Lone Hotel
 What it’s all about: bold design, a lively atmosphere, and a location between Rovinj’s pine forest and the Adriatic. 
In Rovinj, Lone Hotel offers a markedly different interpretation of Istria. Set beside Zlatni Rt forest park and close to the Adriatic, it puts contemporary architecture, design and art at the forefront. Its distinctive Y-shaped form, often compared to a passenger ship anchored in Lone Bay, gives every room view of the surrounding landscape, while the central atrium organizes the public spaces. Conceived as a showcase of Croatian creativity, Lone brings together architecture, design and art, including Ivana Franke’s Room for Running Ghosts in the lobby.
Despite its modern character, nature remains central: pine forest surrounds the property, while the coast and Rovinj’s historic center are within easy reach. The culinary experience also reflects its surroundings, with Tekka by Lone using an extra virgin olive oil created in collaboration with Istrian producer Chiavalon.
A firmly seaside setting at the Grand Park Hotel. © Grand Park Hotel Rovinj by Maistra Collection
Grand Park Hotel Rovinj by Maistra Collection
 What it’s all about: A polished, One-Key waterfront hotel that combines views of Rovinj’s old town with contemporary design, wellness and MICHELIN-Star dining. 
For travelers looking for a comprehensive coastal stay — with design, gastronomy, wellness and easy access to Rovinj — Grand Park Hotel Rovinj has it all, with a striking position across the bay from Rovinj's historic center. Its interiors give the property a sophisticated contemporary character, but the setting remains firmly Adriatic. Large windows and open spaces make the waterfront views part of the hotel experience rather than simply a backdrop.
Several restaurants and bars, including MICHELIN-Two Stars 
Agli Amici Rovinj
 and MICHELIN-One Star 
Cap Aureo
, alongside extensive wellness facilities, give guests plenty of reasons to linger. At the same time, a range of curated experiences bring Rovinj and its surroundings into focus, from boat rides and guided cycling with the General Manager to evenings introducing guests to local producers, authentic products and the people behind them.
Set directly on the waterfront. © Valamar Riviera Hotel & Residence
Valamar Riviera Hotel & Residence
 What it’s all about: A relaxed waterfront base for experiencing Poreč’s historic center and the everyday rhythm of an Istrian coastal town. 
In the heart of Poreč, Valamar Riviera Hotel & Residence offers perhaps the most direct connection to the town life of these five hotels. Rather than sitting tucked away in the countryside or within a resort complex, Valamar’s variety of hotel spaces — ranging from a boutique hotel building to elegant villas — sit directly on the waterfront, with the historic town center just outside.
Here, the town itself becomes part of the hotel experience, where guests may step out of their rooms and into Poreč's narrow, restaurant and café-lined streets. And plenty of culture, besides: from Roman remains and Venetian architecture to the Euphrasia Basilica. The waterfront, meanwhile, provides an immediate connection to the Adriatic.
