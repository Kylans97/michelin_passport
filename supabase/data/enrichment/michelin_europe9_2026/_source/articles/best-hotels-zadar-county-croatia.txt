Croatia’s Zadar Region: 4 of the Best Hotels on the North Dalmatian Coast
Defined by centuries of history and a dramatic Adriatic coastline, Croatia's Zadar Region offers a compelling blend of heritage, nature and hospitality.
                            Croatia
                            Hotels
                            Travel
Share
Inspectors have selected dozens of outstanding hotels across Croatia. But Villa Nai 3.3 – the country’s only Three-Key hotel – makes its home in the Zadar Region, a stretch of northern Dalmatian coast with no shortage of the cultural heritage and rich natural landscapes that have helped make this country one of the Mediterranean’s most popular destinations. 
The setting of the villa is one of the many secluded islands that dot the Zadar Region, and its construction — built directly into the topography — is as strong an example as any of the sense of place that defines Zadar. Elsewhere in this compact region, it’s the medieval cities, dramatic mountains and fertile plains that draw travelers.  
At the four hotels below, you’ll find a range of all the above, from historic fortifications to vibrant city streets and tranquil coastlines.

Bastion Heritage Hotel
  What it's all about: Where ancient walls meet the sea, an Old Town hotel set within the remains of a historic fortress dating to the medieval period. 
Housed within a former fort with roots in the medieval period and rebuilt under 16th-century Venetian rule, Bastion Heritage Hotel offers one of the most immersive ways to experience Zadar's rich history. Set in the city’s historic core, it both preserves its setting’s maritime and architectural heritage while seamlessly blending the feel of ancient stone walls with that of contemporary refinement. Its privileged location allows guests to move effortlessly between the narrow streets of the city centre and the waterfront promenade.
Inside, elegant rooms pair historic character with modern comforts, creating an intimate boutique atmosphere. From the nearby Roman Forum and medieval churches to the celebrated Sea Organ and Greeting to the Sun, many of Zadar's most iconic landmarks are just a short stroll away. The hotel's Kaštel Restaurant celebrates Dalmatian cuisine through seasonal ingredients and local flavours.

Hyatt Regency Zadar
  What it’s all about: A contemporary gateway to the Adriatic, set a short walk from Old Town and steps from the waterfront and marina. 
Fun fact: the hotel occupies the restored Maraska complex, home to the historic distillery that produced the famous Maraschino liqueur, one of Zadar's best-known exports. With such pedigree, it’s only natural that the historic sites are all just a short stroll away, while the hotel itself overlooks the waterfront and marina.
Whether exploring historic streets, setting off on a boat excursion through the Zadar archipelago or taking a traditional ride with the local 
barkajoli
, the traditional boatmen who have connected the waterfront with Zadar's old city walls for generations, guests enjoy an undeniably local perspective. The hotel also offers versatile event spaces, suitable for conferences, celebrations or private gatherings, and dining concepts that showcase a variety of culinary styles, from Mediterranean cuisine to international flavours.
A spa, indoor and outdoor pools, and the Adriatic itself anchor the experience. © Falkensteiner Hotel & Spa Iadera

Falkensteiner Hotel & Spa Iadera
  What it’s all about: Modern comfort immersed in the landscape, with one of the largest wellness centres on the Croatian Adriatic spanning more than 6,000 square metres.
Located in Petrčane, within the Punta Skala resort complex just outside Zadar, Falkensteiner Hotel & Spa Iadera makes the Adriatic the centrepiece of the guest experience. Panoramic sea views, contemporary design and direct access to the coastline create a seamless connection between architecture and nature, while the hotel's location offers the perfect balance between exploring Zadar's historic heritage and enjoying a peaceful seaside escape.
At the heart of the resort is the spa, featuring indoor and outdoor pools, thalasso experiences, panoramic saunas, steam baths, relaxation areas and a wide range of Mediterranean-inspired treatments. Beyond wellness, guests enjoy a private beach, tennis and other sports facilities, fitness activities and outdoor experiences. The culinary offering combines Alpe-Adria influences with Mediterranean flavours, highlighting fresh seafood, local ingredients and carefully crafted dishes inspired by the region.
Villa Nai 3.3 was built through the excavation of the interior of a hillside on the island of Dugi Otok. © Villa Nai 3.3

Villa Nai 3.3
  What it’s all about: A Three-Key retreat with a deep connection to the landscape and a very special design.
Reaching Villa Nai 3.3 on the island of Dugi Otok marks a transition from the energy of urban Zadar to the openness and serenity of island life. As producers of their own award-winning olive oil, Villa Nai 3.3 and its architecture are deeply connected to the land, inviting guests to slow down and disconnect completely, and to rest within low-profile structures carved directly into the hillside.
The experience extends beyond the admittedly remarkable accommodations, through a dedicated spa offering exceptional treatments, including massages by a Bali-trained wellness expert, as well as a fitness centre, private tennis court, cigar room, helipad, outdoor infinity pool and indoor hot tub, private beach area and an olive oil boutique.
The dining experience is unique in its own right: Grotta 11.000, an intimate 18-seat venue inside a natural cave, showcases open-fire cooking with seafood, lobster and organic meats, while 3.3 offers contemporary Mediterranean tasting menus accompanied by a carefully curated selection of mainly Dalmatian but also international wines.
