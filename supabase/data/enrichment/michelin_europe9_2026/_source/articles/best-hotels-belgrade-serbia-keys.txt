The Story of Belgrade Through Its 4 Best Hotels
It's been a strong decade for hotels in Belgrade. Here, four Inspector favorites reveal the history and resurgence of the Serbian capital. Plus, a beloved mountainside bonus.
                            Travel
                            Hotels
                            Belgrade
                            Serbia
Share
Belgrade is among the oldest cities in Europe, a place where block-to-block shifts from Byzantine to Brutalist design can be head-spinning. And while for a long time it was the global mega-chains that dominated the city’s hospitality scene, plopping themselves down among the city’s myriad ancient sights, the last decade of hospitality in the Serbian capital has begun to catch up to its historic pedigree: an indication of just how much creative energy is swirling across the country.
Below, the history of the city, as told through the lens of its best hotels, the

 — with one quick pitstop in the mountains.

The Bristol Belgrade
  What it's all about: In the city center, a newly reopened, Art Noveau classic that harkens back to the elegance of its early 20th-century roots.
One look at the building and it's clear that the One-Key Bristol Belgrade is a splendid example of Art Nouveau architecture. Opened in 1912, the hotel was a hotspot in the 1920s and 30s, when it hosted Rockefellers and British royals.
 Refreshed and reopened in 2025, today it is a landmark in the Savski Venac district, among the oldest and most central sections of the city. A stay here is just over half a mile from Old Town (and sites like the Belgrade Fortress that date back two millennia), while the hotel itself is all elegant, modern comfort — from the creamy shades of the design to the pilates and yoga classes at the spa. A nice touch is the ability to take meals in the tree-lined inner garden.

Square Nine Hotel 
  What it's all about: A completely refurbished building, now sporting a smart Bauhaus facade and MICHELIN-selected dining in the heart of Old Town.
In Belgrade's ancient Old Town, the Square Nine Hotel opened in 2011 with a mission: to stand out from the international chains then dominating the city. Clearly, it succeeds, in no small part due to Brazilian architect Isay Weinfeld's refurbishing of a dilapidated building into a bold Bauhaus exterior filled with mid-century modern furniture sourced from American and Scandinavian designers.
And yet, it still blends into its picturesque setting in Studentski Trg square. Beyond the design, you'll find on site two MICHELIN-selected restaurants, 
The Square
 (for European fine dining) and 
Ebisu 
(for Japanese fare overlooking the historic quarter). Worth a mention too is the wellness area, with is 18-meter pool, state-of-the-art fitness center, sauna, Turkish bath and a Japanese 
ofuro
 soaking tub.

The St. Regis Belgrade
  What it's all about: On the first floors of a brand-new skyscraper, high-end service along the city's waterfront. 
Nothing announces renewal like the raising of a new, towering, glass skyscraper. On the Belgrade Waterfront, itself a recent renewal project to bring residences and public spaces to an area along the Sava River, the St. Regis claims nine floors of a 551-foot tower, newly opened in 2025 and clearly among the most luxurious options in the city.
Inside, it's a One-Key urban retreat with 119 rooms and suites featuring typical St. Regis luxury touches, none so impressive, perhaps, as the signature butler service. For the wellness crowd, enjoy a spa, pool and fitness center. And there's no better way to get acquainted with the city than taking in its skyline from above — a 360-degree observation deck located at the top of the building offers sweeping panoramic views, although it is independently operated and not part of the hotel itself.

Saint Ten Hotel
  What it's all about: One of Belgrade's best boutique hotels hosts high-end comforts in what was once a momentous political space. 
Steps from the must-see Temple of Saint Sava — which began construction in 1935 and finished only recently — the building that holds the Saint Ten Hotel itself dates back many years, to a 1929 construction by an architect, Stojan Veljkovic, whose family were deeply involved in the political scene. In its first life, the stately space was entwined with more than a few pivotal moments in the city’s political history. According to one rumor, MI6 agents visited frequently. 
These days, Saint Ten is one of MICHELIN Inspectors' favorite boutique hotels in the city, where behind the historic facade (and new modern addition), a One-Key hotel represents a devotion to chic contemporary design. Comforts include spa with sauna, as well as the MICHELIN-selected restaurant, 
L'Adresse
, with its European-influenced cuisine and plush, cozy lounge.
Bor hotel by Karisma brings a touch of modern style to Serbia's traditional mountain region. © Bor hotel by Karisma

  Bonus: 
Bor Hotel by Karisma
  What it's all about: A modern take on the beloved Serbian mountain experience in Zlatibor.
Once you've gotten a feel for Belgrade, take a scenic drive some three-hours to Zlatibor, a mountain region with a history as a leisure escape that dates back centuries. Today, Belgraders go both for winter sports and summer hiking, and among a host of the region's traditional lodges, BOR Hotel is among the most modern. 
Opened in 2023, the design is plush and smartly contemporary, but it's the amenities and the mountain setting that make the experience. Enjoy wine tastings and a cigar bar, plus a spa with a selection of Finnish, Bio and Infrared saunas. One restaurant offers international cuisine with ingredients sourced from nearby farms, while the other serves Asian-fusion fare on the rooftop.
